Setting up

Yawn records meetings on this Mac only.

Audio, transcripts and search stay on this machine. There is no account, no server, and nothing is uploaded.

Two permissions are needed before a meeting can be recorded: your microphone, and the meeting audio your Mac plays. macOS asks for each one separately.

First, your microphone.

This is the half of the meeting you speak. Without it a recording holds only the other people.

Now the audio your Mac plays.

This is the other people on the call. macOS calls it system audio recording.

There is no way to check this one without asking: macOS reports it only when a recording is actually attempted, so pressing the button below is the check.

macOS is not showing that prompt again.

Once a permission has been refused, asking a second time does nothing visible. It has to be changed by hand.

Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Microphone, and switch Yawn on. Then come back here and check again.

This screen does not open System Settings for you. Doing that would need a permission to launch other applications, which this window deliberately does not have.

Leaving opens the rest of the app. Recording will not work until the permission is granted, and it will say so when a meeting is started.

How setup will work

  1. Record two short sessions of you talking, at least an hour apart.
  2. Play a stretch of speech that is not you — licensed or public audio — so the app can measure the difference.
  3. Review the measured options and choose one. Nothing is chosen for you.

Setup recordings and their transcripts are deleted once the private profile is stored.
